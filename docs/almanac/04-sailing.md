Learn the wind, learn your canvas, and keep the bow where you want it.

## The wind
One wind blows for every soul at sea. It veers and freshens slowly through the day; rain and thunder stiffen it. Read it from the white streaks on the water, from flags and from bellying sails.

> Aboard, a pale arrow on your compass rose shows where the wind comes from. The longer the arrow, the stronger the wind; it glows amber in a gust.

## Square sails
show_me: sails_and_winch | Show me: rigging a sail
A square sail hangs between two {item:pirates_n_ships:yard}**yards**, rows of yard blocks laid across the ship. The middle block marks the mast.

Both yards stand one block forward of the mast, in the column in front of it, never in the mast's own column. So the cloth hangs before the mast, as on any ship at sea.

1. Set the upper yard across the ship, its middle block just forward of the mast.
2. Set a second yard the same way, 2 to 8 blocks straight below it, with only air or mast between their middles.
3. The pair is now one sail. You place no canvas: the cloth is drawn between them.

## Running and reaching
A square sail loves a wind from astern and is useless close to the wind. Longer yards set further apart make a bigger sail, and three yards on one mast make two sails.

> Click a yard that heads no sail and it tells you what is wrong with the rig.

## Fore-and-aft sails
A triangular sail is rigged with {item:pirates_n_ships:rope}**rope** and three {item:pirates_n_ships:cleat}**cleats**. It is the sail for a wind on the beam.
{recipe:rope}
{recipe:cleat}

1. Put one cleat high on the side of the mast: the head.
2. Put one further forward and lower, on the deck or a bowsprit: the tack.
3. Put one straight below the head: the clew.
4. Use a rope on the head cleat, then on the tack cleat. A stay runs between them and the cloth fills the triangle.

> A rope between two cleats or mooring rings that is no stay becomes a sagging rope line, for a rail or rigging.

## Trimming the canvas
illustration: sail_winch
Every sail has three trims: furled, half and full. Use the {item:pirates_n_ships:sail_winch}**sail winch** on deck to work every sail at once, or click an upper yard or a head cleat with an empty hand to work that sail alone.

No sail drives straight into the wind. A sail that shakes and hangs slack is in the no-go zone: bear away. Your crew braces the yards for you.

## The helm
show_me: steering | Show me: steering at the helm
Hold right-click on the {item:pirates_n_ships:helm}**helm** to take the wheel, then move the mouse or hold A and D to turn: right or D to starboard, left or A to port. Let go and the wheel stays put.

The line above your hotbar shows the rudder angle. The rudder bites only while she moves through the water, and works the other way round when she goes astern.

## Keeping her upright
Wind on the sails heels her over and the hull rights itself; near her limit the sails spill their wind. A wide, shallow hull stands stiffest.

She is ballasted as you built her. What you load later can make her list: trim the cargo and pump her dry, as [[pirates_n_ships:trade|Cargo and trade]] explains.

## Anchor and capstan
illustration: capstan
show_me: anchor_and_capstan | Show me: dropping and weighing anchor
The {item:pirates_n_ships:capstan}**capstan** works the anchor, which hangs outside the hull on its side. Use it to let go, and again to weigh.
{spotlight3d:block:capstan}
{recipe:capstan}

Once the anchor bites, she swings to lie head to her chain. Over water too deep for the chain, the capstan refuses to let go.

>! A ship pulling harder than her anchor holds drags it. Furl before you anchor.

## Oars
show_me: oars | Show me: rowing
{spotlight:oak_oar_bench}
In a calm or against the wind, whistle **Row**: every hand not at a station takes an {item:pirates_n_ships:oar}**oar** at the rail, the sides in turn, and pulls her along at up to 3 blocks a second. A hand seated on an **oar bench** pulls a quarter harder.
{recipe:oak_oar_bench}
{recipe:oar}

The same entry then reads **Ship oars**. Any other order ends the rowing too, and the hands ship oars themselves once the sails are faster.

## Mooring
show_me: mooring | Show me: mooring alongside
A moored ship is simply a ship lying still at her berth: bring her in slowly and drop the anchor.

{item:pirates_n_ships:mooring_ring}**Mooring rings** and cleats serve the grappling hook. A hook catches on a ring and holds longer there; use a ring or cleat on your own ship to make a hooked ship fast. See [[pirates_n_ships:combat|Weapons and combat]].
{recipe:mooring_ring}

## The gangway
The {item:pirates_n_ships:boarding_plank}**boarding plank** bridges your deck and the quay. Use it on the edge of either, toward the other: it lies as one straight board, up or down to a high pier, up to six blocks.

It goes where the ship goes, but let her drift along the quay and it breaks. Sneak and use it with an empty hand to stow it.

## Waves and weather
Fair weather brings a low swell, rain rough seas, a thunderstorm high crests. A small boat rides them far more than a big one.

>! In rough seas every open hatch or low rim near the waterline takes water. Close your hatches and man a pump before a storm.

## Ratlines
illustration: ratlines
{item:pirates_n_ships:ratlines}**Ratlines** are rope nets for climbing aloft.
{recipe:ratlines}

- Click the side of a mast to hang a net like a ladder.
- Click the deck or the gunwale to lay a sloped net, rising the way you face.
- Click a ratline to add the next one to its run. Sneak to place against the clicked side.

A net needs something to rest on: break one and those hanging from it fall too.

## Ratline nets
show_me: ratline_nets | Show me: stringing ratline nets
{spotlight:ratline_net}
A {item:pirates_n_ships:ratline_net}**ratline net** runs from a {item:pirates_n_ships:chainplate}**chainplate** on the rail to a {item:pirates_n_ships:mast_hoop}**mast hoop** up the mast. Use the net on one, then on the other; the line between them must be clear.
{recipe:ratline_net}

Walk into the net and hold forward or jump to climb; sneak to hold on. String several chainplates to one hoop and the space between them is netted too.

## The crow's nest
illustration: crows_nest
show_me: crows_nest | Show me: keeping the lookout
The {item:pirates_n_ships:crows_nest}**crow's nest** is a barrel for the masthead, set on a log, fence, wall or any solid top. Climb in over the rim; sneak to stay put.
{recipe:crows_nest}

## The lookout
Whoever stands in the nest keeps the lookout, you or a hand you post with the whistle. The lookout calls what is new to everyone aboard:

- **Ships,** by their flag: merchant, navy, pirate, wreck or just a sail.
- **Land,** the nearest coast.
- Sharks, and the kraken.

Each call rings a small bell and lights a diamond on your compass: white for land, red for a pirate, blue for the navy.

## The ship's panels
While you are aboard, two panels watch over her:

- **The compass,** bottom left: heading, wind, speed in knots and rudder angle.
- **The hull strip,** bottom right: one cell per compartment, filling blue as water comes in, with a red mark at a breach.

Her name, load and crew are on the ship screen at the helm; see [[pirates_n_ships:ships|Ships]].
