illustration: cutlass
Sooner or later someone will want your ship, your cargo or your head. Learn the blade, the pistol and the great guns, and learn them before you need them.

## Swords
Three blades are worth carrying, each with its own temper:

- {item:pirates_n_ships:rapier}**Rapier** (two iron ingots and a stick): light and long, made for the thrust.
- {item:pirates_n_ships:cutlass}**Cutlass** (three iron ingots and a stick): heavy and wide, made for the slash.
- {item:pirates_n_ships:saber}**Saber** (two iron ingots, a gold ingot and a stick): the officer's blade, good at both.

With one of these in hand the mouse fights differently:

- **Slash:** a quick left click. A wide, short arc after a brief wind-up.
- **Thrust:** hold left click a moment and release. Narrow, longer reach, harder, but slow to recover if it misses.
- **Guard:** hold right click. It stops every blow from the front, but each blow costs stamina, the heavier the dearer. When you cannot pay, your guard breaks and you stagger. Blows from behind or the side ignore your guard.
- **Parry:** a quick tap of right click just before a blow lands. The blow glances off, the attacker staggers, and for a second you may **riposte** for extra harm. A parry at nothing costs stamina and locks your parry for a moment.

Your stamina shows as a brass-framed bar while you hold a sword. Attacks, guarding and missed parries drain it; it refills when you rest. With an empty bar you can neither guard nor parry.

>! While you hold one of these swords, right click belongs to the blade. Put it away to open a door or take the helm.

## Reading your foe
Pirates fight with the same swordplay you do. Watch for the raised arm before a slash, or the arm drawn back before a thrust, and parry just before the blow. A pirate sometimes feints: he stops his swing to bait an early parry and strikes while your guard is spent. Navy officers are skilled duellists with the saber.

## Pistol and musket
illustration: pistol
The {item:pirates_n_ships:pistol}**pistol** and the {item:pirates_n_ships:musket}**musket** (each two iron ingots, a flint and a plank) are single-shot flintlocks. The musket hits harder and flies flatter and truer; the pistol scatters.

1. **Load:** with {item:pirates_n_ships:lead_shot}**lead shot** (two iron nuggets make four) and gunpowder in your pack, hold right click: three seconds for the pistol, five for the musket. Let go early and nothing is lost.
2. **Aim:** with a loaded gun, hold right click. After a second of steady aim the shot is tighter, and the musket draws your eye in a little.
3. **Fire:** left click, while aiming or from the hip. From the hip the ball scatters wide.

Letting go of right click never fires; the gun stays loaded. Sneak to lower an aimed gun. A loaded gun shows a full gold bar in your hotbar and says "Loaded" in its tooltip.

>! In the rain a quarter of your shots misfire with a click; the charge stays in.

## Cannons
illustration: cannon
show_me: cannon_loading | Show me: loading and firing a cannon
A {item:pirates_n_ships:cannon}**cannon** is cast from two iron ingots and a block of iron on a carriage of two logs and a plank. Click the deck block where the **front** of the carriage should stand: the rear takes the block toward you and the barrel overhangs one block ahead, so click the spot by the rail. Both halves need a block beneath them.

1. **Load:** use it with gunpowder, then with a {item:pirates_n_ships:cannonball}**cannonball** (four iron ingots make two). Powder first, always.
2. **Aim:** sneak and use it with an empty hand. The upper half of the block raises the barrel, the lower half lowers it, from 5° down to 20° up in steps of 5°.
3. **Fire:** use it with an empty hand.

The barrel needs five seconds to cool before fresh powder goes in. A ball hits hard and smashes the wooden block it strikes; a hole below a ship's waterline lets the sea in. A ball that grazes a hull at a shallow angle glances off; a square hit does the most harm. From a moving ship the shot carries her speed, and each shot pushes your ship back a little.

> A cannon cannot turn sideways. Lay your ship so her broadside bears.

## Chain shot and grapeshot
Both load like a cannonball, after the powder.

- {item:pirates_n_ships:chain_shot}**Chain shot** (two iron ingots and a chain make two): two balls on a chain. It flies slower and shorter than a ball, breaks no hull and rips through rigging. Through a square sail it tears the cloth, so the sail draws only with what is left; it cuts ropes and stays and breaks ratlines. Torn cloth mends by itself, a block a minute.
- {item:pirates_n_ships:grapeshot}**Grapeshot** (eight iron nuggets in a string-tied bag make two): bursts from the muzzle as nine pellets in a narrow cone. Each pellet hurts whoever it hits and breaks nothing, and none strike your own deck. Fire it across a crowded enemy deck before you board.

## The swivel gun
illustration: swivel_gun
show_me: swivel_gun | Show me: the swivel gun
The {item:pirates_n_ships:swivel_gun}**swivel gun** (three iron ingots over a stick) mounts on a fence, a wall, iron or brig bars, or any full block.

Load it with gunpowder, then a cannonball. Hold right click with an empty hand: the gun follows your eye while you hold, and fires when you let go. It hits less hard and less far than a cannon, breaks no planks and is ready again in three seconds. Sneak and use it to hear what it needs.

## Gun crews
Post a hand at a gun with the whistle. Put gunpowder and shot in a chest, barrel or cargo crate within 4 blocks of the gun on the same ship, then blow **Load!**. On **Fire!** every manned, loaded gun goes off ("Aye, firing!"), and the crew reloads by itself from the supply, so a manned gun keeps firing as fast as it cools.

**Fire at will** lets the crews choose their own targets; see [[pirates_n_ships:crew|Crew]].

## The grappling hook
illustration: grappling_hook
The {item:pirates_n_ships:grappling_hook}**grappling hook** (three iron ingots and a string) bites into whatever it strikes: another ship, your own ship, or the land.

- **Throw it:** right click. Its rope reaches 32 blocks.
- **Shoot it:** put the hook in your off hand and a musket in your main hand. Hold use to load it into the musket (a full reload and a gunpowder), aim and fire. Its rope reaches 64 blocks.

It slips off water, leaves and glass panes, and from creatures, and comes back after two seconds. Throwing a second hook frees the first. Sneak and use with an empty hand to let go. The rope parts if it is stretched past its length.

- **Hooked into another ship from your own:** the rope hauls both ships together until they lie side by side.
- **Haul by hand:** with the rope in hand, hold sneak and its length freezes. Walk back and it pulls the ship toward you.
- **Climb:** right-click your own rope while it is in your hand and you go up it hand over hand, when the hook is at least two blocks above you.
- **Make fast:** use a cleat or mooring ring of your own while your hook is out, and the rope is tied there.

## Boarding
show_me: boarding | Show me: boarding an enemy
Once your rope is made fast to a cleat or ring, look at it and use it: you hang from it and slide down to its lower end, riding along as both ships move. Sneak to let go. From your masthead down onto an enemy deck is the classic way across.

Or lay a {item:pirates_n_ships:boarding_plank}**boarding plank** (three wooden slabs over two iron nuggets). With the ships alongside, use it on the top of your gunwale facing the other ship. Up to six blocks of plank run out and come down on her deck; if her deck stands higher or lower, the plank climbs or falls on stepped ramps, two blocks at most. Keep the ships together: if her end slips more than a block and a half aside, the plank breaks and drops back to you. Sneak and use it with an empty hand to take it up again, from either end.

> Hold the plank and look at your rail: the run it would lay shows in green before you commit, and a red cell shows what stands in the way.

## Taking a prize
Ships sail the sea lanes between the ports even when no one is watching, and when you come within sight of one she is there: a merchantman with goods in her hold and a few armed sailors, a navy patrol, or a pirate under the Jolly Roger. Navy and pirate sloops carry four guns, loaded, and a shot locker by the mast.

- Take goods from a merchant's hold while you stand aboard her, and you have plundered her.
- Sink her, and the deed is yours if your shot was the last to strike her.
- Kill every fighter aboard and hold her deck for a few seconds, and she is yours, crew and all.

>! Taking a merchant is piracy in the navy's eyes. See [[pirates_n_ships:law|Law]].

## Foes ashore and afloat
illustration: mob_pirate
- **Pirates,** in dark coats and bandanas, attack you and the navy on sight. They drop doubloons and now and then a cutlass.
- **Navy soldiers,** in blue coats with white cross belts, carry muskets. They leave you be unless you are wanted; then they keep their distance, aim and fire. They drop lead shot and gunpowder.
- **Navy officers,** in gold-trimmed coats, are deadly with the saber.
- **Sailors** never fight; they run from danger.

Pirates and the navy fight each other wherever they meet. Striking or killing navy men is a crime; killing pirates is not.

## Pirate captains
illustration: pirate_captain
Every pirate island has a captain, a named villain with a standing bounty of 300 doubloons on every notice board. He is a dangerous swordsman.

To fight him alone, sneak and use him with a sword in hand. If he accepts, his crew keeps out of the duel unless you strike them. The duel ends when one of you falls, when you run, or after five minutes. Strike him first and he refuses: there is no honour in it.

Slain, he drops his hat, maybe his coat, breeches and boots, a purse of doubloons, a treasure map of his island and your bounty proof. Taken alive in shackles, he pays better still. Five days after his fall a successor takes his place. Now and then he puts to sea in his own ship, and if you carry a letter of marque or a bounty proof, he hunts you.

## Raids
Linger long at a navy outpost or a seafarer village and the pirates take notice. One day the cry goes up, "Sails on the horizon!", and the bells ring. Pirate sloops come in from the nearest island, drop anchor off the quay and put their fighters ashore. Kill them all and the raid is beaten off; either way the settlement is safe for five days afterward.

## Dress for the part
illustration: captains_hat
Hats and coats are armour too.

- **Hats,** one point each: the {item:pirates_n_ships:pirate_hat}**pirate hat** (three black wool over leather, a bone and leather), the {item:pirates_n_ships:bandana}**bandana** (red wool, string, red wool), the {item:pirates_n_ships:navy_hat}**navy tricorn** (with white wool), the {item:pirates_n_ships:officer_hat}**officer's bicorne** (with a gold nugget), and the {item:pirates_n_ships:captains_hat}**captain's hat**, which you only win from a pirate captain.
- **The captain's coat, breeches and boots,** in charcoal and gold, and **the officer's coat, breeches and boots,** in navy blue and white: three points for a coat, two for breeches, one for boots. All are crafted from wool, leather and a touch of gold.

## Monsters of the deep
illustration: kraken
The **kraken** lurks in the deep ocean and rises beside ships, more often at night and in storms. Its tentacles grip the hull and drag her down, beat masts to splinters and sweep the deck; swimmers are pulled under.

- Its eyes are its weak spots: they take triple harm.
- Enough harm to a tentacle cuts it, and it lets go until it grows back.
- Wounded badly, it sinks away.

It leaves a {item:pirates_n_ships:kraken_beak}**kraken beak** and {item:pirates_n_ships:kraken_ink}**kraken ink**, prized by chart makers.

## Sharks
illustration: shark
Sharks cruise every ocean a few blocks under the surface. A shark that spots you swimming circles before it charges and bites, and blood in the water drives it into a frenzy. Stay on deck or in a boat and it leaves you alone; climb out and it loses you at once.

## Waterspouts and whirlpools
In a thunderstorm at sea a **waterspout** may form: a turning column of spray with a low roar. Close to it you, your boat and loose things are pulled in and lifted, and the wind tears set sails down a step every few seconds. Steer clear, or furl first.

In the deep ocean, at any hour, a **whirlpool** may open: a slow ring of foam around a dark centre that draws everything inward. Near its heart it drags boats and swimmers under, and a ship caught there is turned about.

> Big ships barely notice either.
