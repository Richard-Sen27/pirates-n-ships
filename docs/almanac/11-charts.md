## Your chart
Every captain keeps a chart of his own, and it draws itself as you sail: the coasts within about 96 blocks are inked in as you pass, with hatched shallows, sandy beaches and the open sea, where the odd sea serpent lurks. Only the seas of the overworld are charted.

Craft a {item:pirates_n_ships:chart}**chart** from paper, leather and a feather, and use it to open your map. Or press **M** with the chart in either hand.

- Drag to pan, scroll to zoom.
- Right-click to place a marker with a name: X marks the spot, an anchorage, a skull, a port or a danger. Click a marker to rename or remove it.
- Your ship shows where you are and which way you head.

## Map tiles
illustration: map_tile
A {item:pirates_n_ships:map_tile}**map tile** (eight sticks around a paper) hangs on a wall or lies on a table. Set several side by side in a rectangle, all facing the same way, and they make one board, up to 8 tiles on a side.

1. Use any tile with your chart in hand: your chart opens with a frame the shape of the board.
2. Drag the frame over the waters you want, pick a zoom, and choose whether your markers go on it.
3. Press **Draw**.

Drawing costs ink: an ink sac or glow ink sac for each tile. A single {item:pirates_n_ships:kraken_ink}**kraken ink** pays for eight.

Later, anyone who has charted more can use the board and press **Update**: what they know fills in the blank parchment, what was drawn before stays, and they pay only for the tiles that changed. **New** draws the board afresh at another place or zoom, and **Clear** wipes it; both ask you to click twice. Look at a tile to see who drew it, when, and where. Break a tile and it keeps its piece of the drawing; set it back and the board is whole again.

## Reading bearings
The lookout in the crow's nest calls in points of the compass from the bow: 32 points to the circle, eight to each quarter.

- **Dead ahead** and **dead astern.**
- **Two points off the starboard bow:** a little to the right of ahead.
- **On the beam:** square off the side.
- **Three points abaft the beam:** behind the beam, toward the stern.

## Treasure maps
illustration: treasure_map
Fences on pirate islands sell blank {item:pirates_n_ships:treasure_map}**treasure maps**, and wreck chests hold one now and then. Use a blank map and it marks the nearest buried treasure that no one has yet dug up; if there is none within reach, it stays blank.

While you hold the map it shows the island with a red X and tells you the way: "NW, 340 blocks". At the X, dig about two blocks into the sand and open the chest. Once the treasure is found, every map of it turns grey and keeps as a keepsake, and the next blank map leads somewhere new.

> Every pirate island has a chest buried beneath two crossed logs. Dig at the cross.

## Wrecks
The ocean floor is strewn with the bones of ships: a broken sloop, a field of scattered cargo, a mast stump with its yard, the stern of something larger. Each holds a chest of ship's stores: doubloons, rum, salted fish, rope, nails, lead shot, now and then a cutlass or a treasure map, rarely kraken ink.

Tall wrecks lie only in deep water; in the shallows you find cargo fields. Take something to let you breathe and see down there.
