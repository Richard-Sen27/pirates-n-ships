A flag is a promise. It tells every ship on the horizon who you are and what you mean to do, and the sea holds you to it. Choose your colours with care, and know what it costs to lie with them.

## The colours
show_me: flags_and_allegiance | Show me: hoisting your colours
- {item:pirates_n_ships:merchant_flag}**Merchant flag** (a stick, two white wool, red dye), or no flag at all: neutral to everyone.
- {item:pirates_n_ships:navy_flag}**Navy flag** (a stick, two blue wool, white dye): the navy and the merchants are friends, the pirates foes.
- {item:pirates_n_ships:jolly_roger_flag}**Jolly Roger** (a stick, two black wool, a bone): the pirates are friends, the navy hunts you, and merchants may strike to you.
- **Any banner:** your own flag, neutral to all. The cloth shows the banner's colour and every one of its patterns.

## The flagpole
A {item:pirates_n_ships:flagpole}**flagpole** is three sticks stood on end; it makes two. The flag hangs downwind from its top, a block high and a block and a half long, so leave it room.

- **Use it with a flag:** the flag runs up the pole and flies after three seconds; you get the old one back.
- **Use it with an empty hand:** strike the colours (the flag comes down but stays on the pole), or raise them again.
- **Sneak and use it with an empty hand:** take the flag down into your hand.
- **Break the pole:** the flag drops.

Stack flagpoles to raise one tall pole, up to 6 blocks. The top flies the flag, and you may work it from any block of the pole. Placing a pole on top takes the flag up with it; breaking one in the middle splits it in two, and the upper part keeps the flag.

## What your flag tells the law
A ship flies the highest flag on her poles.

- **Under the Jolly Roger** the navy fires on everyone aboard on sight, and being seen under it is a crime. Pirates let you be.
- **Under the navy flag** you pass the navy, as long as your record is clean and the navy thinks well of you. A suspect who flies it may be found out by any navy soldier in sight. Then you are charged heavily, and the navy hunts your ship for five minutes whatever she flies.
- A navy ship with a manned crow's nest sees your flag half again as far and sees through it half again as fast.

>! Firing on a ship that has struck her colours, or on one under a merchant flag or a banner, is a crime. If your crew fires, the charge is yours as her owner.

## Striking your colours
Strike your flag and you surrender: the navy and the pirates stop attacking. A navy patrol that sees you strike holds its fire, keeps you in sight for half a minute and sails on. Raise the Jolly Roger again and the chase is back on. The [[pirates_n_ships:law|Law]] chapter tells you what your crimes cost.

## Banners and dyed sails
Hoist any banner on a flagpole to fly your own flag: the cloth takes the banner's colour and patterns, its top edge at the pole.

Your canvas can carry colour too.

- Use a **dye** on the upper yard of a square sail, or the head cleat of a triangular one: the whole cloth takes the colour, and one dye is spent. White dye brings back the plain canvas.
- A large square sail can carry a **banner**: use the banner on its upper yard. The cloth takes the banner's colour with its patterns drawn upright over it. Both yards must be at least 3 blocks long and 2 blocks apart.
- Sneak and use the yard with an empty hand to take the banner back; breaking the yard block that holds it drops it. While a banner hangs, dyes are refused. Triangular sails take dye only.

Colours are only for show: they never change what a sail does, and they stay through launching, taking apart and every change of trim.
